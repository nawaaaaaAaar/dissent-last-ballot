# Delhi setting and visual references

## v0.5 geographical anchors

Delhi Tourism places Jantar Mantar on Parliament Street in Connaught Place and identifies Patel Chowk as the nearest metro station. [Delhi Tourism](https://delhitourism.gov.in/tourist_place/jantar_mantar.html).

The observatory's Samrat Yantra triangular sundial and Rama Yantra open-cylinder forms inform the original in-game geometry; they are not scanned meshes. [Jantar Mantar, New Delhi overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar,_New_Delhi).

The protest gathering is placed on the street side of a fenced observatory compound. Reporting describes the road opposite the observatory as the demonstration space. [National Herald's description of the protest site](https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/amp/story/politics/jantar-mantar-is-a-police-cantonment-no-freedom-of-expression-here-any-more).

These sources anchor place and form, not every building position, distance or event. The playable road/courtyard/assembly area is a compressed authored level, not GPS-accurate geography or real protest footage.

v0.2 replaces the generic-city setting with a Delhi-grounded art pass. Real place names and public-facing equipment appearance are sourced; gameplay positions, distances, characters, signs, clashes, and the archive are dramatised.

## Route and streets

PTI reported an October 3, 2026 march from Patel Chowk towards Jantar Mantar. [PTI via ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/aisa-activists-return-to-jantar-mantar-around-150-protesters-detained/3061032/?amp)

Delhi Tourism identifies Jantar Mantar's location as Parliament Street, Connaught Place, and Patel Chowk as its nearest metro station. [Delhi Tourism](https://delhitourism.gov.in/tourist_place/jantar_mantar.html)

The Wire's October 2 reporting names Sansad Marg, Janpath Road, and Tolstoy Marg in the public protest context. [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/government/security-beefs-up-before-jantar-mantar-protest-against-cec-metro-stations-closed)

Question of Cities describes the Jantar Mantar protest street as a tree-lined boulevard connecting Tolstoy Road and Ashoka Road, with wide pavements. [Question of Cities](https://questionofcities.org/jantar-mantar-apart-delhis-streets-and-borders-are-now-temporal-protest-spaces/)

**Game interpretation:** Three named stages, Patel Chowk → Sansad Marg → Jantar Mantar Road, use a compressed 900-game-metre layout. That number is not a measured walking distance. The street is not a GPS reconstruction; building layouts, obstacle placements, traffic, and the fictional archive are not real-world navigation or deployment information.

The new environment uses a tree-lined verge and boundary fence on one side, pale institutional/colonnaded architecture on the other, and original wayfinding props. These surrounding facades are illustrative, not replicas of identified buildings.

## Delhi Police barricades

The public-facing barricade reference is Sidheeq's Wikimedia Commons photograph, identified as a Delhi Police barricade at Parliament Street police station. Its licence is CC BY-SA 3.0. [Wikimedia Commons photograph and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Delhi_Police%27s_Barricade.jpeg)

A 2024 ethnographic paper describes Delhi's movable metal barricades as bright yellow with red labelling. [Riddhi Pandey's conference abstract](https://nomadit.co.uk/conference/easa2024/paper/78777)

**Game interpretation:** Original 3D models show a yellow metal frame and mesh, red “DELHI POLICE” lettering, and small wheels. Proportions are approximate game art, not a technical drawing. The reference photo is retained at `reference-barricade.jpeg` as a reference only; it is not applied as a game texture. Credit: Sidheeq, own work, uploaded September 16, 2017, CC BY-SA 3.0, unmodified downloaded file. Its licence applies to the photograph, not as a claim that all project code has that licence.

Low construction hurdles remain separate game objects; they are not depicted as full-height Delhi Police barriers that can simply be jumped. The fictional scripted collapse is not a verified reconstruction of any reported incident.

## Bus

The Indian Express's October 3 report describes protesters being moved into buses near Jantar Mantar, and its main image was used privately as a visual reference. [The Indian Express report](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/delhi/jantar-mantar-protest-yogendra-yadav-kapil-sibal-neha-bora-detained-cec-gyanesh-kumar-10905302/)

**Game interpretation:** An original dark-body transport-bus model has guarded windows, metal trim, and an amber stripe, drawing on the observed image. The added “DELHI POLICE” label is illustrative; the image does not verify that exact side label, manufacturer, registration, or all-around livery. No identifiable detainee is modelled, and no copyrighted news photograph is bundled into the game or repository. This replaces the previous generic damaged bus with an intact, source-referenced vehicle.

## Protest

India Today reported October 2 slogans concerning “vote chori,” “kanun chori,” and examination paper leaks. [India Today](https://www.indiatoday.in/india/video/vote-chori-protest-youth-congress-hits-delhi-streets-over-paper-leak-and-eci-ytvd-3008387-2026-10-02)

**Game interpretation:** A protest tableau contains 24 original fictional participants and original placards, including “VOTE CHORI BAND KARO,” “SAVE DEMOCRACY,” and journalist-accountability messages. It is not a reproduction of a photograph, an exact crowd count, or a claim that every written slogan was documented verbatim. No real activist or journalist's likeness is used.

The allegations and inquiry status concerning journalists remain separately cited in the main README. The game does not re-enact sexual assault or represent an unresolved allegation as an adjudicated finding.

## Observatory

Public references describe the Samrat Yantra as a triangular sundial with quadrants to either side. [Jantar Mantar, New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantar_Mantar,_New_Delhi)

**Game interpretation:** A scaled red-stone Samrat-Yantra-inspired silhouette sits behind the park fence near the final stage. It is approximate scenery, not a scan, a conservation model, or an exact-scale reproduction. The runner never enters or damages the monument.

## What “actual” means in this build

The place names, protest reporting, and visual references are real. The 3D assets are original approximations, and the route and story are fictionalised. Exact photogrammetric assets, surveyed geometry, real protest footage, and real-person likenesses have not been added.
