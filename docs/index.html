DISSENT

The Last Ballot.

A proposed realistic 3D runner about carrying evidence through a city that wants it silenced. Fast movement. Ordinary people. A public truth worth protecting.

Pre-production, not gameplay.
This is a research-backed concept, not a playable game or finished 3D artwork. The title and setting remain proposals.
The premise

Run for something.

You are a fictional student courier. Your mission is to get a verified evidence packet from a printing shop to an independent archive at a public assembly. A city that initially feels hostile gradually becomes a network of solidarity.

The position is clear: pro-resistance, anti-authoritarian, anti-fascist, critical of government repression, and opposed to vote theft. The action is nonviolent. No guns, revenge missions, or attacks on civilians.

Not a coin chase with political stickers. A courier story where the politics shape the purpose of every run.

Proposed first mission

One route.
Real stakes.

Scope target: 90–120 seconds. None of these features is implemented yet.

  1. Leave the printing shop

    Start at dusk. Read three lanes, move, jump, and slide. The evidence packet replaces a generic score chase.

  2. Choose the market passage

    A fictional road closure creates a branch. A bookseller opens a route through shutters and delivery carts.

  3. Make the handoff

    Help another courier duplicate the packet at a safe point. Trade time for solidarity, rather than treating people as collectibles.

  4. Reach the public assembly

    Deliver the packet, verify receipt, and reveal the people waiting at the destination. Accountability is the reward.

Art direction

Grounded.
Not generic.

The visual target is a lived-in, Delhi-inspired city: worn plaster, painted shutters, believable signage, warm shop interiors, restrained dusk lighting, and streets that remain readable at speed.

  • Characters: properly rigged humans, convincing weight shifts, animation transitions, and feet that do not slide.
  • World: coherent materials, architectural scale, licensed environment assets, and purposeful set dressing.
  • Camera and sound: controlled chase-camera motion, optional shake, spatial footsteps, original music, and subtitles.

These are acceptance criteria, not claims about a build. No pre-rendered image should be passed off as playable graphics.

Initial research

The real-world context.

“Jatamantek” is provisionally interpreted as Jantar Mantar; “ochori” as vote chori. Please confirm before literal-location production.

Jantar Mantar / September 25

National Herald reported detentions at an AISA and civil-society protest over alleged electoral-roll irregularities, and quoted police saying permission had not been obtained. Read the National Herald account

Vote chori protest / October 2

India Today reported a Youth Congress demonstration from Jantar Mantar towards Raisina Road, using election cards and slogans about alleged vote theft and other grievances. Read the India Today account

Allegation is not adjudication

The New Indian Express reported opposition allegations alongside the Election Commission's position that its orders have legal sanction and follow statutory procedures. The game should not turn disputed accusations about real people into proven facts. Read the claims and official position

Recommendation: fictional characters and institutions, with an optional, separately sourced real-world context archive. No private voter data, unlicensed protest photographs, or real-world evasion instructions.

AI production research

Models help.
Pipelines matter.

OpenAI shows Astra game examples and describes Blender-to-Unreal scene work. A secondary MindStudio account describes a full art-to-engine workflow, but explicitly calls its results unfinished and not production-ready. OpenAI examples / Workflow and limitations

Anthropic reports strong graphics and polish in an early Opus 5.5 game comparison, without a named game or reproducible scores. Creator-posted Fable 5.1 examples include kart games, Three.js shooters, and a subway FPS; these are demonstrations, not independent release-quality tests. Anthropic's account / Fable creator roundup

The useful sequence is art target → modelling and materials → rigging and animation → engine assembly → repeated play-testing → measured optimisation. No other model was run to build or evaluate this concept.

Next production gate

Prove one beautiful street.

Before expanding the world, make one approved street module and one animated courier work in-engine. Measure frame pacing on named devices, then build the full mission loop with start, pause, failure, restart, and finish.

Browser-first is the recommendation for easy sharing. Native-first is a separate option if maximum visual fidelity matters more than opening a link. Neither platform has been benchmarked for this project.
